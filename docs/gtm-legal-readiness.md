# Go-to-Market Legal & Admin Readiness — Selling GEO to Small Businesses

> **Not legal or tax advice — general information to orient you.** Two founders,
> Berkeley/CA, pre-revenue, cold-emailing/calling SMBs with websites to sell GEO audits +
> retainers. Every dollar figure and CA-specific rule should be confirmed with a CA
> attorney/CPA and the City of Berkeley directly. **[LAWYER]/[CPA]** = genuinely worth a
> professional's sign-off. Researched July 2026; laws change.

## The short answer to "do we need to file as a business before reaching out?"

**No — you do not need to form/register any entity to cold-email, cold-call business lines,
run free audits, or book calls.** By default you're operating as a business already (see the
partnership nuance below), and no state/federal formation filing gates *outreach*. **But**
two things are true and matter more than "do we need an entity":

1. **The real rules on outreach are marketing-compliance rules, not formation rules** — and
   a couple carry serious teeth in California (see Phase 1).
2. **One local license *is* required once you operate/earn** — the City of Berkeley business
   license (a small tax), not tied to sending a first email (see Phase 2).

## The two-founder nuance (know this)

A "sole proprietorship" is single-owner. **Two people carrying on business for profit = an
automatic general partnership** — no filing needed, but **unlimited personal liability, and
each partner is personally liable for the other's business acts.** That's the main reason a
2-founder shop forms an **LLC sooner** than a solo founder would. You can *start* as a
partnership; just know you're personally exposed until you form an entity. **[LAWYER]** on
entity choice + a founder/operating agreement.

## Phase 1 — Before / for outreach (what's actually required)

**Formation required: none.** Compliance by channel, cleanest → riskiest:

### ✅ Cold EMAIL — lowest-risk first channel. Every email must contain:
- **Accurate From / sender name + a real sending domain** (no spoofing).
- **A truthful, non-misleading subject line.** ← biggest CA litigation lever (below).
- **Honest identification** of who you are and that you're offering a commercial service.
- **A valid physical postal address** — a **USPS PO box or a CMRA/virtual mailbox** is fine;
  it does **not** have to be your home address.
- **A working one-step opt-out** (unsubscribe link / "reply to opt out"); keep it live ≥30
  days; **honor opt-outs within 10 business days** (aim for ~2), never re-email or sell them.
- Governing law: **CAN-SPAM** (FTC) — no B2B exemption; up to ~$53k/email. **Plus California
  BPC §17529.5**: a **private right of action, ~$1,000 per email, near-strict-liability** for
  *false/misleading From or subject lines*, actively litigated by plaintiff firms. You're in
  CA emailing CA businesses, so this is squarely in scope → **be scrupulously honest in From
  + subject; no fake "Re:"/clickbait.** **[LAWYER]** if you ever get a demand letter.
- Infra to set up first: a **separate sending domain** with **SPF+DKIM+DMARC** (Gmail/Yahoo
  require it), the PO box/CMRA, a **suppression list**, low daily volume + domain warm-up.
- Sourcing lists: emailing a **publicly listed business address** is fine; using providers
  (Apollo etc.) is legal; avoid mass automated address-harvesting/dictionary attacks (an
  *aggravated* CAN-SPAM violation). You remain the "sender" regardless of data source.

### ⚠️ Cold CALLING — OK to start manually, but ONE real open question
- **B2B calls are generally exempt from the National DNC registry** (it protects consumers).
  Safe lane: **a human manually dialing curated, genuine business lines**, honest pitch, honor
  "don't call again" immediately, keep basic records (the 2024 TSR extended no-misrepresentation
  + recordkeeping to B2B).
- **TCPA cell-phone trap:** many SMB "business" numbers are **personal cells**, which the 9th
  Circuit (governs CA) treats as **presumptively residential**. Using an **autodialer (ATDS),
  ringless voicemail, or any prerecorded/AI voice** to a cell without consent → **$500–$1,500
  per call, no cap.** → **No autodialers, no prerecorded/AI voice. Manual dial only.**
- **🚩 California telephonic-seller registration (§17511.1) — TOP consult-a-lawyer item.** CA
  requires "telephonic sellers" to **register with the CA AG ~10 days before soliciting, pay
  $50, and post a $100,000 surety bond.** CA's exemptions are enumerated (no clean general B2B
  carve-out), and whether a small B2B marketing cold-caller falls in scope is **genuinely
  ambiguous.** The $100k bond is a material decision. **Resolve this with counsel before
  running a calling program.** (Calling businesses in other states — AZ/FL/NY/OH/TX/PA etc. —
  can trigger *their* registration/bonds too.)
- **Call recording:** California is **all-party consent.** If you record, **announce it at the
  start of every call** and proceed only if they stay on; otherwise just take notes.

### ❌ Cold TEXTING / SMS — avoid at launch
TCPA applies ($500–$1,500/text, no cap); **A2P 10DLC registration requires a legal entity/EIN
and assumes prior opt-in**; cold/unsolicited SMS has no safe-harbor and gets campaigns
suspended. Don't cold-text. Use SMS only after opt-in.

**Verdict for Phase 1:** Start with **compliant cold email** — no entity needed, lowest risk.
Manual B2B calls are workable but **get the CA §17511.1 registration/bond question answered
first.** Skip texting.

## Phase 2 — Before you sign a client & take money

| Item | Required? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **City of Berkeley business license** (a tax) | **Yes, tied to operating/earning** | Due within **30 days of "commencing business activity"** in Berkeley; ~**$76/yr** for a small service biz ($51 min tax + $25 reg). Home-based → also a **Home Occupation permit / Zoning Certificate**. **Confirm the "commencement" trigger + your tax class with Berkeley Finance (510-981-7200)** and Planning (510-981-7410). |
| **Signed services agreement** | **Yes, before paid work** | Scope/deliverables, fees + payment terms, term/termination, IP (transfers on payment), **limitation of liability (cap at fees paid)**, and a **no-guarantee-of-results / warranty disclaimer** (critical for GEO — you can't control what ChatGPT surfaces). Start from a reputable template (Rocket Lawyer etc.), then **[LAWYER]** a one-time ~1hr review of the liability cap + no-guarantee + IP clauses (reusable across all clients). |
| **Payment rail** | **Yes** | **Stripe/PayPal "individual/sole-proprietor" account works with just your SSN** — no LLC needed. Invoicing/ACH supported. You can collect money this week. |

## Phase 3 — Soon after / before scaling

- **Form an LLC** once deals are real. It's the genuine **liability shield** (important given
  per-email/per-call statutory-damages exposure) *and* the strongest "we're a real company"
  signal. Cost is why you wait: **CA's $800/yr minimum franchise tax** (first-year exemption
  has **expired**), plus ~$70 filing. **[CPA/LAWYER]** on entity choice + $800 timing.
- **E&O / professional liability insurance** — not needed for deal #1; buy it (~$500–1,500/yr)
  when a client contract requires a certificate or deal sizes grow.
- **Estimated quarterly taxes + self-employment tax (15.3%)** — set aside ~25–30% of net
  profit; pay quarterly if you'll owe $1k+. **[CPA]**.
- **CCPA/CPRA** — does **not** apply to you yet (thresholds ~$26.6M revenue or 100k CA
  consumers). Revisit only if you scale hugely.
- **Multi-state telemarketer registration** — if/when you cold-call businesses outside CA.

## The three things that could actually bite (consult-a-lawyer flags)

1. **🚩 CA telephonic-seller registration + $100k bond (§17511.1)** — the only item that could
   *block* a cold-calling program. Get an answer before you dial at volume.
2. **CA anti-spam §17529.5** — $1,000/email strict liability for misleading From/subject lines.
   Mitigation is free: be honest in every header. But it's real money if ignored.
3. **General-partnership personal liability** (two of you, no entity) — you're each on the hook
   for the other's acts and any of the above penalties until you form an LLC.

## Cheapest legitimacy wins (the "is this legit?" fix)
Domain email (not gmail) → one-page site + SSL → **the free audit itself as proof/case study**
→ LinkedIn for both founders. These convert cold B2B prospects; an LLC helps but is a
when-you-have-revenue move.

## Recommended starting sequence
1. **Now:** buy a domain + set up SPF/DKIM/DMARC on a separate sending domain; get a PO
   box/CMRA; write the compliant cold-email template; stand up a one-page site + domain email.
2. **Before dialing:** get the CA §17511.1 registration/bond question answered. **Lead with
   email** meanwhile.
3. **Before first paid deal:** Berkeley business license + home-occupation; signed services
   agreement (lawyer-reviewed once); EIN + business bank account; Stripe.
4. **Once deals are real:** form the LLC; add E&O when required.

## Correction to a flag raised earlier in the packaging docs

Earlier research flagged reports that **Anthropic tightened its terms in 2026 to bar serving
external paying customers on a shared company key**, and treated it as a possible threat to
the licensing model. **Verified: the story is real but was misread, and it does not apply.**

- What actually changed (~Feb 2026) was a restriction on **OAuth tokens tied to Claude
  Pro/Max consumer subscriptions**, shutting down tools that piggybacked third-party agent
  traffic onto a personal flat-rate subscription. **Standard pay-per-token API-key usage was
  untouched.**
- Anthropic's Commercial Terms §A.1 affirmatively permit the exact pattern here: Anthropic
  "gives Customer permission to use the Services, **including to power products and services
  Customer makes available to its own customers and end users**."
- §D.4 bars reselling *the Services* — i.e. raw API access — which is a different thing.

The same shape holds across all four AI vendors: **selling a derived report product built on
your own server-side keys is the intended commercial pattern and needs nothing extra.**
OpenAI's Services Agreement §2.2 grants the right to "integrate the Services into Customer
Applications and to make Customer Applications available to End Users"; §3.1 separately bars
reselling account access or sharing credentials.

**The one line not to cross, and it becomes live with the agency deal:** never hand the
agency (or its clients) a raw API key, and never let their developers author their own
prompts against your key as if it were their account. As long as they only ever see finished
reports or your software's output, you are inside standard terms and do not need BYOK or a
reseller agreement. Revisit only if the agency wants to pay its own inference costs directly.

The audit publishes Perplexity Output — verbatim excerpts of what `sonar` said about a

No other vendor requires attribution: nothing found for Anthropic or OpenAI; Gemini has only a

## ⚠️ Vendor risk: the two SERP intermediaries

This is where the real exposure sits — and it is **contractual and operational, not a code
problem**. DataForSEO and Serper scrape Google on your behalf.

**Does that risk flow down to you?** Less than the vendor's, but not zero, and it changes
shape rather than disappearing:

- **Direct liability is low.** You never accessed Google directly and never accepted Google's
  ToS, so you're not in privity. Post-*hiQ*/*Van Buren* the CFAA effectively doesn't reach
  public, unauthenticated data regardless of who fetches it; contract claims run against the
  scraper.
- **The category's legal terrain just improved.** Google sued SerpApi (Dec 2025) on DMCA
  §1201 anti-circumvention grounds. On **20 July 2026** the court **dismissed the DMCA claims
  for ordinary search results outright**, holding public search results aren't copyrighted
  material, with a narrow window to re-plead limited to **Knowledge Panels** (which may embed
  licensed third-party content).
- **But that carve-out points straight at our most exposed surface.** The unresolved
  Knowledge-Panel theory is the closest analogue to AI Overviews / AI Mode content — exactly
  what `dataforseo_ai_mode.py` pulls. Watch it.
- **The realistic risks are spillover and continuity**, not being a defendant: discovery
  naming downstream users, and a vendor being blocked or forced to change methods, breaking
  the pipeline with no notice.

**DataForSEO's indemnity runs one way — against you.** §7.2 requires *you* to indemnify
*them* for any use of SERP data that violates a search engine's terms; there is no
vendor→customer indemnity. §7.1 bars using SERP data "to compete with or adversely affect the
business interests of the search engine providers." Live-mode results are **not retained by
them at all** — you must store what you fetch. Nothing in their terms addresses agency
resale either way.

**Serper's terms are notably thin** — no data-use section, no retention clause, no reseller
carve-out. Indemnity is *mutual* (better than DataForSEO on paper, untested in practice), but
the licence is terminable **"at any time, with or without cause, with or without notice."**
That is a genuine continuity risk to hand an agency licensee.

**Industry practice, for calibration:** Semrush, Ahrefs and seoClarity do not publicly
disclose SERP sourcing or its legal basis anywhere in their customer terms. The absence is
itself informative — the category has collectively decided not to surface this. That's a
pattern, not a safe harbour.
